---
title: Every shortcut
lede: The Mac's keys, in the groups the app's own Keyboard Shortcuts sheet uses.
---

The app lists every shortcut in its Keyboard Shortcuts sheet. Press <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>/</kbd> to open it, or <kbd>?</kbd> in the reader, or choose Help › Keyboard Shortcuts, or choose "Keyboard Shortcuts" in <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd>. Type in its Filter field to narrow the list. The tables below follow the sheet's groups and order, and use its names for each action.

The reader's single keys (<kbd>J</kbd>, <kbd>K</kbd>, the numbers, <kbd>N</kbd>, <kbd>B</kbd>, <kbd>C</kbd>, <kbd>R</kbd>, the arrows and <kbd>Esc</kbd>) act on the selected verse while the reader has focus. The reader takes focus when a chapter opens, when a note is finished, and when <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> or the Keyboard Shortcuts sheet closes. While <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> is showing, or a note is being edited, these keys do nothing, so what you type there never acts on the verse. Held down, a number, <kbd>0</kbd>, <kbd>N</kbd>, <kbd>B</kbd>, <kbd>C</kbd> or <kbd>R</kbd> acts once. <kbd>J</kbd>, <kbd>K</kbd>, <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>J</kbd>, <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> and the arrows repeat.

## On the iPhone

With a hardware keyboard, <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> opens Search from any tab, and <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>[</kbd> and <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>]</kbd> go back and forward on the Read tab. The rest of this page is the Mac's.
