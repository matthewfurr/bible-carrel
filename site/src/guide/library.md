title: The Library
lede: The books of the Bible, and what the app knows about their people, places and dates.
---

On the Mac the Library starts at Home, the page the app opens on. On the iPhone it opens from the Library button at the left of the reader's bar, and lists the same books. A book opens to its chapters and to four buttons: BibleProject's overview video, People, Places and About. All four are there for each of the 66 books. The books of the Apocrypha have no People, Places or About, because the data does not cover them.

## Home

Every book is listed under its Testament and its group, such as Law, History and Poetry & Wisdom, in as many columns as fit. [Your first chapter]({{ '/guide/first-chapter/' | url }}) covers choosing a book and a chapter with the pointer.

{% shot "home", "Home on the Mac: this week's reading plan above the books under each Testament, with John chosen and its chapters in a pane at the right", "lazy", "(min-width: 65rem) 43rem, 100vw" %}

From the keyboard, <kbd>Tab</kbd> goes to the books and the arrow keys move between books and columns. <kbd>Return</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> moves into the chapter pane, where the arrows move between chapters and <kbd>Return</kbd> opens one. <kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>F</kbd> goes to the toolbar's search of the books. <kbd>⇧</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>H</kbd> comes back to Home from anywhere, with the book you are reading chosen. <kbd>Esc</kbd> leaves a People, Places or About view for the list of books.

## Rings

A chapter with a highlight, note or bookmark has a ring around its number, on both apps. The ring has one segment for each highlight color in the chapter, up to the first six. A chapter marked without a color has a plain gray ring.

## People

People lists everyone the data names in the book, each with a count. A switch at the head of the list orders them Most named, with the most verses first, or A to Z. This device remembers the choice for every book.

{% shot "people", "The People view for Genesis, with Jacob chosen from the list of names and his family drawn as a tree of parents, partners and children", "lazy", "(min-width: 65rem) 43rem, 100vw" %}

A person's page opens with their name and, under it, the data's own description, such as "son of Boaz". Where the data has none, nothing is shown. Then come their facts: father and mother, each of which opens that person, what else they are called, when they lived, how many verses name them here and in all, and the size of their family. The dictionary entry, from Easton's Bible Dictionary (1897), shows its first paragraph. Show more opens the rest and Show less closes it.

On the Mac the family is a drawn tree: grandparents, parents, the person with their siblings, then the children grouped under each partner or named mother. When a generation is too wide for the column it folds into a count such as "7 siblings", which opens in place. On the iPhone the family is a line down the timeline's rail, with the household branching below. Every name opens that person. Apart from the data's own descriptions, the app never writes "son", "brother" or "eldest", because the data records neither sex nor birth order.

The verses that name the person are listed one line to a chapter, for example "3 1-2, 5-6, 8-10, 12, …", in this book and in the other books they appear in. A click on a run of verses shows them in place, with Open. Open goes to the reader, and Back returns to the same person.

## Places

Places shows Apple's map framed to the book's places, with a list beside it. On the iPhone the list is a sheet. The map needs a connection for its tiles. The pins and the list do not. Places the data cannot locate are listed under "Not on the map", and a position the data marks as rough is called approximate.

{% shot "places", "Places in Acts: Apple's map with a pin for each place, beside a list of places by how often they are named, with the map's attribution and the data credit below", "lazy", "(min-width: 65rem) 43rem, 100vw" %}

The list comes in three orders: Most named, A to Z and By chapter. This device remembers the choice. By chapter lists the places under a heading for each chapter that names any. Choose a heading and the map follows, framed to that chapter's places, with the book's other places as small dots.

## About

About gives the book's name and subtitle, then its facts: Testament and group, chapters and verses, the writers the data names, the years it spans, and the five most named people and places. A line such as "and 347 more" opens People. Under them is the book's timeline, in the order of history. A life is one row, such as "lived 930 years", and an event with children heads a group, as the Flood does in Genesis. On the Mac a chart of the book's whole span sits over the timeline: click it and the list moves there.

## Dates

The years follow a traditional chronology, Ussher's as worked out by Floyd Nolen Jones, which puts Creation at 4004 BC. A notice says so the first time a year is shown. Turn off Show dates in Settings › General, under Library, to hide every year, along with the chart and the spans. Lengths, such as "lived 930 years", stay.

## By name and coming back

<kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>K</kbd> on the Mac, and Search on the iPhone, find a person or a place by its name, or by a name it is also called, and open its page for the whole Bible. See [Search and ⌘K]({{ '/guide/search/' | url }}).

Back and Home return from a passage opened in the Library to the same book, view and person or place. [Your place]({{ '/guide/your-place/' | url }}) says how long that lasts and what makes the passage your place instead.
