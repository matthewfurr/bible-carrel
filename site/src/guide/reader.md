title: The reader

The reader shows one chapter and stops at its end. There is no scroll through a whole book. Everything that says "this chapter" follows the chapter you have open: the window title, the notes column, Ask About This Chapter and, on the iPhone, Notes in This Chapter. So does your place.

## How the page is set

The chapter is set like a printed page, as far as each translation's text allows. A paragraph indents its first line, except the chapter's first paragraph and one that follows poetry. Each line of poetry is its own paragraph and hangs under its first word when it wraps. A verse that ends a stanza is followed by half a line of space.

The translations mark paragraphs and poetry differently, so the same passage can look different from one to the next. The World English Bible marks paragraphs and stanzas. Some translations mark poetry lines but not paragraphs.

## The opening and the running head

Chapter 1 opens on the book's title and subtitle, then its own label: CHAPTER 1 in Printed, the book's name and the number in Study and Classic, and a drop numeral in Lectern. A later chapter opens straight on its text in Printed, Study and Classic, and on its numeral in Lectern.

The running head sits at the top of the window and names the chapter. It is hidden while chapter 1's title is in view. The thin line under it, in the accent color, grows as you read and is full at the chapter's foot. It is empty when the whole chapter fits in the window. Click the running head to go back to the book's chapters.

## Previous and Next

At the end of the chapter, its links go back or on: "Previous: John 2" at the leading edge and "Next: John 4" at the trailing edge. When they do not fit side by side, one sits above the other. At a book's ends they name the neighboring book's chapter, such as "Next: Acts 1" at John 21.

On the Mac they appear as the chapter's foot scrolls into view. <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>⌥</kbd><kbd>⌘</kbd><kbd>→</kbd> do the same from the keyboard, and with the reader focused <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>→</kbd> turn chapters too.

## The book thumb

Along the right edge, the book thumb holds the whole book like the edge of a ruler: one tick for each chapter, every fifth longer, with the chapter you are reading marked in the accent color. The ticks swell under the pointer.

On the Mac it appears when the pointer comes near the right edge, and for a moment after you land on a chapter, but never because you scrolled. It stays for two and a half seconds and then fades out slowly. Drag it to choose a chapter. A loupe beside the pointer shows the chapter number. When you let go, the chapter opens at its start.

## On the iPhone

The running head is the title of the bar at the top of the screen. The book thumb is the right edge of the screen: touch and hold there, then drag. You feel a tap as it takes hold and another for each chapter you pass, and the loupe stands to the left of your finger. When you lift your finger, the chapter opens.

Past each end of the page is the chapter switcher, a stack of chapter cards:

1. Pull up past the end of a chapter, or down past its top. The cards come into the space the pull opens.
2. Let go once you feel the tap. The front card shows the next chapter, or the previous one at the top, with its number and opening verses.
3. To bring a card further along to the front, flick the page the way you pulled. Flick the other way to step back.
4. Tap the front card to open its chapter.

Past a book's last chapter the stack ends on a title card for the next book. The cards stay until you tap one or tap the page.
