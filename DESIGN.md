# Design System: Bible Carrel

## 1. Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Study Carrel"**

A carrel is a desk in a library: a lamp, a shelf, books open at once, a chair you can sit in for an hour. Nothing on it is decorative and everything is within reach. The app is that desk. The chapter is the open book in the middle, set with a real measure and a baseline rhythm; the contents page, the book pane, the notes column and the notes browser are the shelf and the notepad at its edge; the toolbar and the ⌘K palette are the lamp switch, used without looking. The study assistant, when it arrives, is a second reader at the next desk with its own voice, never the author.

Density is Mac-native and honest: a three-pane reading app in the Reeder and NetNewsWire line, with Things' discipline about whitespace and one owned hue, and Craft's care that a note renders like a document. Surfaces are tonal, not shadowed: the page is the brightest thing in light mode and the quietest in dark mode, and panels sit one step away on a slate-tinted paper scale. The reader's own highlight colors are the only saturated color on the page; the verse numbers and the chapter label carry a quiet bronze ink, as a printed Bible's rubrication does, and the app's verdigris accent lives in selection, the current chapter and the icon, and stays out of their way.

What this system rejects, from PRODUCT.md: the web page in a window (flat white, hyperlink feel, chapter as a scrolling div); the SaaS dashboard (gradients, card grids, stat tiles, material as decoration); and the default SwiftUI look the app has today (gray-fill-plus-hairline cards, nested bordered editors, fixed-point chrome type, stock pastel highlights, numbers chosen by feel).

**Key Characteristics:**

- Page-first: the chapter column sets measure, margins and rhythm; every other surface scales from it.
- Tonal depth on a slate-tinted paper scale; shadows only under the one floating panel.
- Two type voices: the page face (Literata by default, chosen by the reader's page style) for anything that is scripture (chapter, book title, section headings, chapter numerals, quoted phrases), SF Pro for everything that is the app.
- Four color roles (Paper, Ink, Accent, Assistant) plus the reader's Marks, each a named Color Set with light and dark values.
- Selection is a wash, never a border. Rows have rhythm, not boxes.
- Motion reports state and nothing else; Reduce Motion turns it into cuts.

## 2. Colors: Verdigris on Slate

Cool slate-tinted paper and ink, a verdigris accent like aged bronze, a dusty plum for the assistant, and the reader's marks at one shared lightness so every highlight reads the same. Every value below is a `ColorToken` in `Palette.swift` with light, dark and (where named) Increase Contrast and Sepia values. SwiftUI reaches it through `extension Color` (`Color.paperPage`, `Color.inkSecondary`, `Color.accentWash`); AppKit drawing uses `Palette.x.platformColor`, a dynamic color that resolves per appearance. Values were chosen in OKLCH and are stored as sRGB hex. `PaletteTests` fails if a pairing below drops under its stated ratio.

The app target's `AccentColor` asset mirrors the `accent` token (with high-contrast variants), so system controls follow it when the reader's system accent is Multicolor. The app's own marks (selection washes, the focused-highlight outline, bookmarks, the current chapter) use the token directly, so they stay verdigris whatever the system accent is.

### Paper (surfaces)

- **Page** (token `paper.page`, light `#F5F7F9`, dark `#101418`): the chapter column, the contents page of the front page, the notes browser's list. The brightest surface in light mode, the deepest in dark mode. Never pure white or black; the slate cast (hue 245 to 250, chroma 0.004 to 0.010) is the whole identity of the neutrals.
- **Panel** (token `paper.panel`, light `#EDF1F4`, dark `#191D22`): the front page's book pane, the notes column, Settings panes, the browser's sidebar. One step from the page in each direction: slightly darker than the page in light mode, slightly lighter in dark mode, so a panel reads as a different surface without a border.
- **Overlay** (token `paper.overlay`, light `#FAFCFE`, dark `#22272D`): the note editor while editing, the assistant's prompt tiles, popover fallbacks, the ⌘K panel (solid, with no material under it) and the keycap's fill in the shortcuts sheet. The lightest step in dark mode, where elevation is lightness.

### Ink (text and rules)

- **Primary** (token `ink.primary`, light `#192028`, dark `#E5E8EC`): scripture, note bodies, titles. 15:1 on the page in both appearances. The reader's `textColor`.
- **Secondary** (token `ink.secondary`, light `#5D646C`, dark `#A0A5AB`): verse numbers, references, counts, help text, inactive symbols. 5.6:1 light, 7.5:1 dark: safe for small text, which is why verse numbers use it rather than tertiary.
- **Tertiary** (token `ink.tertiary`, light `#8D9399`, dark `#6D7277`): dates and other metadata that is never the only channel for information. Below 4.5:1 by design; never for text a reader must read. One more use, by exception: in Places' By chapter list the rows of the chapters other than the chosen one dim their name and detail to `ink.tertiary`, as the front page's search dims the books that do not match; they stay in the list and a click chooses them, and each chapter's heading names it, so the dimming is never the only channel.
- **Rule** (token `ink.rule`, light `#D3D8DC`, dark `#292E34`): hairlines, the editor border, the dot's keyline, the palette's stroke. The only border color in the app.
- **Verse bronze** (token `ink.verse`, light `#7A5320`, dark `#DDB683`): verse numbers and the chapter label in the Printed page style (the default). A low-chroma warm ink, the page's rubrication, not a second accent: it never appears in chrome. Verse numbers sit on highlight fills, so it holds 4.5:1 on the page and on every mark fill in both appearances (light: 6.3:1 on the page, 4.8:1 on Orange, the lowest; dark: 9.8:1 on the page, 4.7:1 on Green, the lowest). Lighter bronzes such as `#9A6B2F` read warmer but fall to 3.3:1 on a mark; `PaletteTests` holds the line.

### Accent

- **Verdigris** (token `accent`, light `#247966`, dark `#73C3AE`): the app's tint. Selection in lists and the palette, the current chapter in the grid and the ring round a chapter a search has found, prominent buttons, the outline around a focused highlight, the field of the app icon (see App icon under Components). 4.9:1 on the page in light mode, so it may carry short text at `.semibold`; it is not a body text color.
- **Verdigris wash** (token `accent.wash`, light `#D1F0E6`, dark `#0A3028`): the fill behind a selected row, card or palette item, and behind the current chapter cell. Primary ink stays above 10:1 on it. Replaces every `Color.accentColor.opacity(0.12)`.
- **The timeline chart's tokens** (About, Mac): lives are bars in `ink.tertiary` and eras bars in `ink.secondary`, so a span reads as an era by its darker bar; event ticks, bar names and the axis are `ink.secondary`; the band is `accent.wash` with no stroke. The chart is never the only channel for a date or a name (the tertiary ink is for marks, not for text a reader must read: the rail beside it has the words).

### Assistant

- **Plum** (token `assistant`, light `#7E517A`, dark `#D3A6CE`): the study assistant's symbol, its name, the label naming which model answered. Nowhere else, so that the assistant is always recognizable and never mistaken for the app or the reader.
- **Plum wash** (token `assistant.wash`, light `#F7E4F5`, dark `#372435`): the fill behind an assistant reply and the chapter prompt menu's header. A reply saved as a note loses the wash and becomes an ordinary note.

### Sepia

A third appearance beside Light and Dark (Settings › Reading › Appearance: System, Light, Sepia, Dark), applied app-wide like them: the light appearance with warm paper and ink. Only seven tokens carry a Sepia value (`ColorToken.sepia`); the marks, `ink.verse`, the accent, the assistant and both washes keep their light values, and system controls stay light (the app's `NSAppearance` is aqua, the window's style `.light`).

- **Page** (`paper.page`) `#F5EDDE`. The mockup's `#F4ECDD`, one step lighter: verdigris on it measured 4.47:1, under the 4.5:1 it holds on every page; at `#F5EDDE` it is 4.51:1.
- **Panel** (`paper.panel`) `#ECE2CF`.
- **Overlay** (`paper.overlay`) `#F9F3E8`.
- **Primary ink** (`ink.primary`) `#2A2118`: 13.6:1 on the page, 12.3:1 on the panel, and above 11:1 on every default mark as in Light (Orange the lowest, 11.1:1); above 10:1 on any user color held to the band. The mockup's `#2B2219`, one step darker: it measured 10.99:1 on Orange.
- **Secondary ink** (`ink.secondary`) `#6A5B49`: 5.6:1 on the page, 5.1:1 on the panel.
- **Tertiary ink** (`ink.tertiary`) `#9A8B78`: 2.8:1 on the page, below 4.5:1 by design as in Light.
- **Rule** (`ink.rule`) `#DCCFB8`.

Verse bronze holds 5.9:1 on the Sepia page and 4.8:1 on Orange. With Increase Contrast, the inks that have a light high-contrast value take it (secondary `#414951`, tertiary `#515961`, rule `#989FA6`) and the papers stay warm. Every pairing `PaletteTests` checks runs in Sepia and Sepia with Increase Contrast as well (`marksStayReadableOnSepia` covers the marks).

Neither platform has a sepia appearance, so the tokens learn it from `SepiaMode`: on macOS a flag the `NSColor` provider reads, with every window taken through the other appearance and back in one turn of the run loop so AppKit and TextKit redraw; on iOS the `SepiaTrait` on every window scene, which the `UIColor` provider reads and which bridges into SwiftUI. SwiftUI colors resolve from `EnvironmentValues.isSepia` (each macOS scene root takes `.followsSepia()`); a platform ink shown in SwiftUI goes through `Color(platform:)`, never `Color(nsColor:)`, which would not change with Sepia. On macOS `Color(platform:)` takes its Sepia from the global flag (`SepiaMode.isOn`, read by the token's `NSColor` provider) and reads the environment's `isSepia` only so that SwiftUI resolves it again when Sepia changes; on iOS it resolves with the environment's `isSepia` as the trait. Every SwiftUI view hosted in AppKit (`NSHostingView`, `NSHostingController`) is its own root and takes `.followsSepia()` itself; `SepiaModeTests` scans the sources and fails on one that does not, unless it is allow-listed with a reason. `AppearanceChoice.apply()` sets the Sepia flag before the appearance and takes the windows through the round trip only when the appearance itself stays the same (Light and Sepia).

### Marks (the reader's highlights)

Seeded defaults for the `HighlightColor` table (`MarkupContainer.defaultColors`), replacing the stock pastel five. On launch, `migrateDefaultColorsIfNeeded` moves any stored default still at its old value onto the new one in place (Pink becomes Rose); a renamed or recolored default is left alone. All five share one lightness (OKLCH L 0.91 light, 0.40 dark) so no highlight is louder than another and primary ink stays above 11:1 on each in light mode and 7:1 in dark.

- **Yellow** ( light `#F8E18A`, dark `#554704`)
- **Green** ( light `#BCF2BD`, dark `#2E522F`)
- **Blue** ( light `#B8E8FF`, dark `#144D6E`)
- **Rose** ( light `#FFCFE8`, dark `#67344E`)
- **Orange** ( light `#FFD0A1`, dark `#683B18`)

User-added colors are stored as hex pairs in SwiftData, held to the defaults' lightness band so the contrast guarantee covers them too. The band (`MarkLightness`) is OKLab L 0.88 to 0.93 for the light variant and 0.37 to 0.41 for the dark. The store enforces it: `addColor` and `setColorHex` keep a variant already inside the band byte for byte and move an out-of-band pick to the nearest band edge at the same hue, reducing chroma until it fits sRGB; on launch, `conformColorsIfNeeded` does the same to any stored color, once. At those edges primary ink stays above 10:1 on a light mark and 6.5:1 on a dark one for any hue (`PaletteTests`); the dark band stops at 0.41 because at 0.43 a saturated green falls near 6.2:1. The Add Color sheet starts a new color at the first default's values and previews the conformed pair as two keylined dots under the pickers, captioned "Kept light enough for the text to stay readable."; the Settings wells show the stored value, so a well set to black lands on the band's gray. Highlights never carry meaning on their own: the column and browser always show reference and text.

### The ring (a marked chapter)

A marked chapter (a highlight, a note or a bookmark in it, in any translation) wears a ring round its number in place of the dot it had. The ring is one rounded rectangle inset `xxs` (4) in the cell, a scaled 1.5 pt line with butt caps. One colour is a whole ring; two to six share it equally with a gap of 0.03 of the path between them, the first starting at the top's middle and running clockwise; with more than six the first six show, in the reader's colour order. A chapter marked with no colour (a bookmark, a chapter note, an uncoloured highlight, or a highlight whose colour has since been deleted) gets a whole ring in `ink.tertiary`.

- **The colour rule.** A highlight's own colour is a fill and too pale to draw a line. The ring takes the colour's hue at OKLab L 0.55 in light appearances and 0.68 in dark ones, with as much chroma as sRGB holds at that hue. That holds 3:1 against `paper.page`, `paper.panel` and `accent.wash` (the cell of the chapter being read), in light, dark and Sepia and at Increase Contrast, for the five defaults and for 24 hues around the wheel (`PaletteTests`, `aChaptersRingHoldsThreeToOneOnEverySurface`; the worst readings are 3.2:1 in light and 3.35:1 in dark). The spec said about 0.65 for dark; at 0.65 some hues fell to 2.98:1 on the Increase Contrast wash.
- **The colourless ring is under 3:1.** `ink.tertiary` reads 2.56:1 to 2.89:1 on the light and Sepia surfaces and 2.95:1 on the dark wash, as the dot it replaces did. `PaletteTests` holds it at 2.5:1. It is a known limit, not a pass.
- **Not colour alone.** VoiceOver reads a marked chapter as "Marked".
- **A custom colour near grey** has an unstable hue, so its ring is drawn vivid. A neutral ring below a chroma threshold is in the backlog.

### Named Rules

**The One Saturated Voice Rule.** On the page itself the only saturated color is a highlight. The accent appears on the page only as the 1.5pt outline around a focused highlight, in the gutter's bookmark glyph (the margin marks on the phone) and in the chapter-note mark on the heading's line. Chrome, selection and the icon are where verdigris lives. Two amendments, by Matt's choice (2026-11-11): the default page style's verse numbers and chapter label are in `ink.verse`, a muted bronze that is ink rather than color; and the page styles the reader can choose instead may put the accent on the page in one named place each (Lectern's chapter numerals, Study's verse numbers). Nothing else on the page takes the accent, and no page style colors the text itself. **The marks have a second home, in the Library**: the ring round a marked chapter's number, on the contents page's pane and in the phone's chapter lists, is drawn in the hues of the highlights the chapter holds. It says "you marked this chapter, in this colour", which is the reader's own data and not the app's tint, so it is not a use of the accent. The rule for the ring's colour is the next heading.

**The No Opacity Tint Rule.** `Color.primary.opacity(0.04)`, `Color.accentColor.opacity(0.12)` and friends are not tokens. Every fill is a palette token. The one exception is the shadow under the ⌘K panel (nothing dims behind it any more, so the scrim that was the other exception is gone).

**The Semantic Red Rule.** Destructive actions (Delete Note, Delete Highlight, Remove Highlight) use the system `.destructive` role and the system red; verdigris and plum never signal danger, and no mark color is used for status.

## 3. Typography

**Page Font:** the page face, set by the reader's page style: Literata by default (bundled, SIL Open Font License: Light, Regular, Italic and Medium for the page, SemiBold and SemiBold Italic for a search's matched words alone), or Iowan Old Style, Source Serif 4 (bundled, OFL) or New York (the system serif design, the face before page styles). See Page Styles below.
**Body Font:** SF Pro (system, semantic text styles)

**Character:** Two voices, kept apart on purpose. Anything that is scripture speaks in the page face: the chapter text, the book title and its subtitle, section headings, the book names on the front page's contents and the chapter numerals in its pane, and a phrase quoted in a note or browser row (these follow the reader's page style, so the quoted words always look like the page they came from). Anything that is the app speaks in SF Pro: labels, references, note bodies, counts, Settings, the palette. The serif marks "this is the book"; the sans marks "this is the desk". A reader can tell at a glance which words are the text and which are theirs. The chapter label ("CHAPTER 1") is the one piece of SF Pro on the page, set as a small spaced label, the way a printed Bible sets its running heads.

### Page Styles

*Decided 2026-11-11 (Matt chose Printed as the default after comparing four mockups, and asked for all four to ship); built in milestone 9. The settings and the heading data source are in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-12-reader-page-styles-and-book-scroll-design.md`.*

A page style is a named set of typographic choices for the chapter: the face, how the book and chapter are introduced, how section headings and verse numbers look. All four ship; the reader picks one in Settings › Reading and Printed is the default. A style is a preset: the reader's size, line spacing and the other reading settings apply on top of whichever style is chosen. Each style is one `ReaderTypography` value plus a heading treatment, so the reader, the phone and the quoted rows all take it from one place. Book titles, subtitles and section headings show only where the translation (or the app's own book table, for subtitles) provides them; a chapter without them reads as it does today, never with an empty gap.

- **Printed** (default). Literata. Book title centred in Literata Light at 2.45 × the reader size; the subtitle ("The Gospel According to John") centred beneath in italic at 0.9 × the size, `ink.secondary`; the chapter label "CHAPTER 1" centred in SF Pro semibold at 0.6 × the size, uppercase, tracked 0.22 em, in `ink.verse`. Section headings in Literata italic at 0.94 × the size, `ink.secondary`, flush with the text, one size of air above (none when the heading opens the chapter) and 0.45 × the size below. Verse numbers in SF Pro medium at 0.58 × the size, raised, in `ink.verse`. Prose paragraphs indent their first line, as today. Line height multiple 1.3 at Normal, as now; Literata's taller x-height carries the extra openness seen in the mockup.
- **Lectern.** Iowan Old Style (system, macOS and iOS), old-style figures. The book name centred in small capitals tracked 0.16 em at 1.65 × the size, the subtitle in italic beneath, then a small centred ornament in `ink.rule`. Each chapter's number is a drop numeral in the `accent`, standing in for verse 1's number; there is no chapter label. It is the page face with its font features cleared and lining figures asked for (`DropNumeral.font`; a face that has no such feature, Iowan among them, keeps its own default figures, which in Iowan are lining; the front page's grid takes the system serif for tabular ones, R15), sized so a figure rises from the first line's capital height to the third line's baseline, its figures proportional and tracked so each stands 0.04 of its size from the next (nearly touching, never colliding; "119" is 124 pt wide at 18 pt, not 161), and set flush with the column's leading edge at the head of the chapter's first paragraph (after its section heading, when it has one). The first three lines step right of it by the figures' width plus half the size; every line beside it starts at that edge, whatever its paragraph's indent, and a wrapped poetry line keeps its hang, and a chapter that opens on short poetry lines wraps them all. Whatever follows a first paragraph shorter than the numeral (a heading) is held below it, with the air it would have after three full lines. Lines beside the numeral do not hyphenate and keep the body's pitch. The numeral is drawn, never text (copying, highlights and search see only the verses); VoiceOver reads it as "Chapter 3", and the headings rotor names the chapter at its opening, a hairline just above the text, with no air above it (section 5, Reader). The chapter-note mark sits in that air at the column's trailing edge. **Ruling R16 (2026-11-12), superseded the same day:** R16 had set the numeral centred on a line of its own above the text, because a non-rectangular text container shortened lines and slowed whole Psalms through the whole book; the container is now non-rectangular only while a chapter's first paragraphs lay out (`DropZoneContainer`), and Lectern's whole Psalms loads and reflows in about a tenth more time than Printed's (253 and 214 ms against 230 and 198, debug). See the milestone 9 plan's Decisions. Section headings centred in small capitals tracked 0.08 em, `ink.secondary`. Verse numbers in Iowan at 0.62 × the size, `ink.secondary`.
- **Study.** Source Serif 4, left-aligned. The subtitle as an SF Pro uppercase label (`.caption` weight semibold, tracked 0.14 em, `ink.secondary`) over "John 1" in Source Serif semibold at 2.1 × the size with the chapter number in `ink.tertiary`, then an `ink.rule` hairline. Section headings in SF Pro semibold at 0.83 × the size, `ink.primary`, like a study Bible's. Verse numbers in SF Pro bold at 0.6 × the size in the `accent`, nearly on the baseline.
- **Classic.** New York, the page as it was before page styles, with the new parts added and nothing else changed: "John 1" in Display, the subtitle in italic `ink.secondary` beneath, section headings in New York semibold at 0.94 × the size, verse numbers in `ink.secondary`.

Every style keeps the page's other rules: the measure, the margins, the poetry indents and stanza air, highlights, the bookmark gutter, the chapter-note mark on each chapter's opening line and the progress line. A style changes how the words are set, never what is on the page.

### Hierarchy

- **Display** (`.semibold`, `Font.system(.title, design: .serif)`, line height 1.1): the book name at the head of the front page's book pane and over the phone's chapters, and the chapter heading in the Classic page style. Replaces `.system(size: 28, weight: .semibold, design: .serif)`. In the other styles the reader's heading is the style's book title and chapter label (Page Styles).
- **Book title, subtitle, chapter label, section heading:** per page style (Page Styles), each derived from the reader size like the text itself.
- **Reader** (`.regular`, the page face at the user's size, 14 to 28 pt, line height multiple 1.3 at Normal, 1.15 Compact, 1.5 Relaxed, paragraph spacing 0.78 of the size, 14 at 18 pt): the chapter text, built by `ReaderTypography.styled(...)` from the page style and the reader's settings. Non-dynamic by design because the reader controls it directly. The measure is proportional to the size (640 pt at 18, 498 at 14, 853 at 24), so a line holds about 70 characters at any size, times 0.85, 1 or 1.15 for the column width setting (Narrow, Normal, Wide). It is a maximum: the column narrows with the window, keeping 48 pt side insets.
- **Verse numbers** (`.medium`, SF Pro at 0.58 of the reader size rounded to the half point, raised 0.39 of the size, `ink.verse` in the Printed style; per page style otherwise): superscripts that disappear until wanted, 10.5 pt at 18 and 14 pt at 24.
- **Chapter numerals** (`.regular`, the page face at `.title3` through `Font.custom(_:size:relativeTo:)`, `.monospacedDigit()`; in a face that cannot set lining figures, which is Iowan Old Style and so Lectern, the system serif, `Font.system(.title3, design: .serif)`, whose figures are lining and tabular: Iowan has no lining-figure feature and its old-style figures ragged a tabular grid, **ruling R15**, 2026-11-12): the phone's chapter lists. **The front page's book pane sets its numerals in the page face at the reader's size** (`PageFace.paneNumeralFont(size:)`: the face's lining-figure font at the reader size, and in New York and Iowan Old Style, which cannot set lining figures, the system serif at the reader size, where the phone's fallback stays `.title3`), in a cell 2.9 by 2.2 times the reader size (52 by 40 at 18), each rounded to a whole point; the phone's grid is unchanged. Current chapter in `ink.primary`, the rest in `ink.secondary`.
- **Headline** (`.semibold`, `.headline`): column and sheet titles ("Notes"), Settings section titles, the notes browser's group headers.
- **Body** (`.regular`, `.callout`): note bodies in the column, browser row bodies, palette row titles. `.body` is reserved for Settings forms and the editor, where the system expects it, and for the two places milestone 11, project 5 set a name in it: a browser row's passage (semibold) and a source's name on Settings › About.
- **Quote** (`.regular` italic, the page face at `.callout` through `Font.custom(_:size:relativeTo:)`, `ink.secondary`; New York's `Font.system(.callout, design: .serif)` in the Classic style): the quoted phrase on a note row, a compact row, or a browser row. The page's voice inside the desk's chrome.
- **Label** (`.semibold`, `.caption`): references ("Matthew 5:3"), translation tags, counts. Numeric labels use `.monospacedDigit()`.
- **Meta** (`.regular`, `.caption2`, `ink.tertiary`): dates only.

### Named Rules

**The Dynamic Type Contract.** Every text style outside the reader is a semantic text style. `.font(.system(size:))` is banned in chrome; the codebase has none. The exceptions are the reader itself, the verse number and the page style's book title, subtitle, chapter label and section headings, all derived from the user's reader size; so are the Mac front page's book names (the reader size), its Testament titles (1.15 times it) and its chapter numerals (the reader size, in the pane), and its group labels (0.58 times it, never under `.caption2`'s size, SF Pro semibold through the platform font, tracked in proportion: 1.4 pt at `.caption2`'s base size and more above it). **The Mac's Library views** (People, Places and About, their lists and pages; Matt's review, 2026-11-24) are the third: every text style is set at its macOS size times the reader size over `.body`'s 13 pt, to the half point (`LibraryType`, passed by the environment from `FrontPageView`), so `.body` is the reader size (18 pt by default) and every smaller role steps down in proportion (at 18: `.callout` 16.5, `.caption` 14, `.largeTitle` 36); at the least reader size (14) nothing is smaller than the system's own style. The text a page is read in (names, facts' values, the dictionary paragraph, the verse lines, a list row's name) is `.body`, the reader size. Sizes and gaps tied to that text (a `@ScaledMetric`'s value) grow by the same scale. The phone's Library and Settings › About keep the semantic styles and Dynamic Type. The count inside the chapter-note mark is an exception too: a `@ScaledMetric(relativeTo: .title)`, so it scales with the paper around it.

**The Scaled Metric Rule.** Any size or spacing tied to text (the color dot's 10 pt, the chapter ring's 1.5 pt line, the swatch chip's and row buttons' 24 pt, the marker column's 14 pt, a row's padding, the phone's chapter cell) is a `@ScaledMetric(relativeTo:)`, never a literal. Three sizes on the Mac's front page left this list in milestone 11, because they follow the reader size rather than Dynamic Type: the contents page's column is the widest book name at the reader size, measured, plus the dot, its gap and the wash's inset on both sides (`ContentsMetrics`); the pane's width is five cells at the reader size, at most 40% of the window, or the width the reader drags it to (`ChapterPaneMetrics`, `PaneWidth`); and the Mac's chapter cell is 2.9 by 2.2 times the reader size (`ChapterPaneMetrics`).

## 4. Elevation

This system is tonal. Within the window there are no shadows: depth is the step between `paper.page`, `paper.panel` and `paper.overlay`, and the hairline `ink.rule` where two surfaces meet at a `Divider`. In dark mode the steps run the other way (panels and overlays lighter than the page) so elevation stays lightness rather than darkness. The macOS 26 window chrome, toolbar, sidebar vibrancy, popovers and sheets are the system's and are not restyled. Liquid Glass appears where the system puts it, and the app applies `.glassEffect()` nowhere of its own. The one glass-like thing it draws is the book thumb's loupe, a lens over the page:

- **The book thumb's loupe** (`LoupeView`, `LoupeLens`, Mac and iPhone; spec 3.3 and 4.4): while the thumb is dragged, a 64 pt (`AppSpacing.xxxl`, scaled) disc beside it holding the chapter number alone, in the page face's lining figures (`DropNumeral.font`, as a drop numeral and the switcher's cards set a chapter's number, in every page style) and the `accent`, at `.title`'s size as a `@ScaledMetric`. It is a disc of clear glass on the page (Matt, 2026-11-13: "I want clear Liquid Glass ... some blurring, but not to that extent"). The number is drawn clear and unbent. Through the glass's middle the page shows as it is, the same size, barely softened (a 0.8 pt blur) under a 15% veil of `paper.overlay`. From 58% of the radius outward the glass curves to its edge and bends the page in more and more, until at the rim what lies beyond the loupe (up to half a radius) is squeezed against the edge, red and blue parting slightly; the bend grows from nothing with no step (`LoupeLens.bend(at:)`), so no line shows where it begins. A thin line of light runs round the inside of the rim, brightest toward the top leading side and opposite it. Only at the middle, right behind the number, the words are quieter (Matt, 2026-11-13: "lighter and slightly more blurred just in the center", the edges kept): a soft pool of the paper's light (64% at the centre) and a stronger blur (2.2 pt), both fading out by 62% of the radius, just where the glass begins to curve, so the rim is untouched. A pale reflection lies across the glass's upper part. An `ink.rule` hairline rims it, and it casts the app's one other shadow (below). The app draws it with Core Image from a picture of the page (and a margin around the loupe) that the text controller takes each time the loupe moves (`PageBackdrop`); the bend is a small Metal warp kernel (`KernelSources/LoupeWarp.ci.metal`, built per platform by `Scripts/build-kernels.sh` into ScriptureUI's resources; 2026-11-16), and the glass is drawn off the main actor, the newest position only (`LensRenderer`). It does not ask the system for glass: at this size the system's clear glass smeared the page to a flat tint on the phone and showed none of it on the Mac. Turned down on the way: a lens that bent only a ring (its inner edge showed), one that only blurred, and a raindrop that magnified and blurred too much to read as glass. Under Reduce Transparency it is the plain number on an opaque `paper.overlay` disc. It is decorative and hidden from VoiceOver; the thumb's adjustable element speaks.

The chapter switcher's cards (section 5, Reader) are paper too: they sit in space of their own with nothing behind them to bend, and what is liquid about them is how they move (Matt, 2026-11-13).

### Material Vocabulary

- **Floating panel** (solid `paper.overlay` in `RoundedRectangle(cornerRadius: 12, style: .continuous)`, no material, 1 pt `ink.rule` stroke): the ⌘K palette, the one surface that floats over content. Nothing behind it dims: a click on the page outside it closes it (a clear, VoiceOver-hidden layer over the window's content takes the click), and the panel carries the modal trait. The layer covers the content, not the toolbar, so a click on a toolbar button acts and leaves the palette open (read in the code, not tried live). It is bright in every appearance and the same under Reduce Transparency, since there is no material to thin.
- **Panel shadow** (`.shadow(color: .black.opacity(0.2), radius: 24, x: 0, y: 8)`): the palette only, cast by the panel's own shape, so no row or keycap inside it casts one. Nothing else in the app casts a shadow.
- **Popovers** (system popover chrome): swatch row, color picker, and the two verse panels (Compare Translations and Cross-References, section 5, Verse panels). Content padded `xs` (8), no extra background; the verse panels are padded `sm` (12) and hang from the selection's first line, under it, with the arrow on that line. Like the others they take the system's own chrome, which is translucent, and add no fill, stroke or shadow of their own.

### Named Rules

**The One Shadow Rule.** The ⌘K panel is the only shadow in the app, but for one small thing: the book thumb's loupe, glass lying on the page, casts a soft one (`.black.opacity(0.14)`, radius `xxs`, 2 pt down) while it shows. A note, a row, a chapter cell, a settings group: none of them floats, so none of them casts.

**The Tonal Step Rule.** A surface is distinguished by which paper it sits on, not by a stroke around it. A `Divider` (`ink.rule`) may separate two panes; a border around a block of content is a sign the structure is wrong.

**The one exception: the chapter switcher's cards.** The switcher stacks same-colour cards (`paper.overlay`) behind one another on the page, and without an edge they would merge into one sheet and into the paper. Each card carries an `ink.rule` stroke of `lineWidth: 1`. The app has no hairline token; every stroke uses a fixed 1 pt (0.5 pt on small circles), so the cards follow that convention rather than `1 / displayScale`.

## 5. Components

### Buttons

- **Shape:** system. Toolbar buttons and the translation pill keep `.bordered` with `.buttonBorderShape(.capsule)`; Settings and sheets use `.bordered` and `.borderedProminent`.
- **Primary:** `.borderedProminent`, tinted by `AccentColor` through the catalog. One per sheet at most (Add Color's Add).
- **Hover / Press state:** system. No custom `ButtonStyle` for standard actions.
- **Inline symbol buttons** (edit, delete, color dot, markdown toolbar): `.buttonStyle(.plain)`, `ink.secondary`, `.help` and `.accessibilityLabel` on every one, 24 pt minimum hit area via `.frame(minWidth: 24, minHeight: 24)`.
- **Destructive:** `Button(role: .destructive)`; system red; confirmation only where the README already promises one (the browser's Delete).

### Rows (notes column, compact highlights, browser, search)

The note card is retired. A note in the column is a row (`NoteRowView`, `CompactHighlightRow`, shared parts in `ColumnRowParts.swift`). Search results are rows too, grouped under headers rather than boxed.

- **Click contract:** the first click selects a row (its highlight is selected, outlined and scrolled to in the reader); a click on a row already selected, Return, or the Edit Note action opens the editor. On a compact row that starts a note. A chapter note has nothing to select, so a click edits it at once. Escape, ⌘Return (or Return, per Settings) or a click elsewhere finishes.
- **Keyboard:** rows are focusable; ↑ and ↓ move between them and select each one's highlight; Delete deletes.
- **Delete:** undoable through the window's undo manager (Edit › Undo, ⌘Z; ⇧⌘Z redoes), so the column asks no confirmation. The trash appears only on the selected or editing row; the context menu and the accessibility actions always offer it.
- **VoiceOver:** each row is one element ("Note, Matthew 5:3, Yellow"), a button with the selected trait when lit, with Edit, Delete and one "Color …" action per swatch.
- **References in notes** (`NoteBodyText`, both apps; spec `2026-11-12-phone-notes-swipes-library-design.md` 3): a note's body reads as rendered markdown with its references as links in the accent, found as it is shown (`ReferenceLinker`'s note rules) and never written into the note. Any reference typed links: a book's name in any case ("gen 1:1", "GEN 1:1"; a lower-case word of one or two letters is a word, not a book), a bare "5:3" in the note's own book, and a chapter alone behind a capital ("Romans 8", "ROM 8"; "mark 2 items" stays prose). On the Mac a click shows the verses in a popover on the note, as a reply's reference does, with Open (the passage in the main window); in the Notes browser a row's first lines carry the same links. VoiceOver reaches each reference as an action named for it. The editor shows plain text.

- **Corner Style:** `RoundedRectangle(cornerRadius: 6, style: .continuous)` for the selection wash only.
- **Background:** none at rest. Selected (its highlight is selected in the text): `accent.wash`. Editing: `paper.overlay` behind the editor field only.
- **Border:** none. Rows are separated by rhythm: `sm` (12) vertical padding, `xs` (8) between the reference line, the quote and the body. In the Notes window only, a 1 pt `ink.rule` hairline (`HairlineRule`) inset `sm` from both edges sits between two notes of a group (none above a group's first or below its last), and goes while either note beside it is selected, so the wash stands alone (its opacity follows the wash, `.snappy`, a cut under Reduce Motion; it keeps its point of height, so nothing moves); between groups the same rule runs the list's full width and always shows, `xxs` under a group's last row, so a selected last row's wash stops 4 pt short of it and never sits on a line (spec 5.3).
- **Internal Padding:** `sm` (12) horizontal and vertical on the column's rows and the browser's rows, scaled with `@ScaledMetric`; search rows are `xs` (8) vertical and `sm` horizontal (below).
- **Anatomy:** marker (10 pt color dot with a 0.5 pt `ink.rule` keyline in a 24 pt hit area, `underline` glyph for a noteless anchor, `note.text` for a chapter note, `bookmark.fill` in the accent for a bookmark), Label reference, Meta date on the trailing edge of the reference line, Quote phrase (two lines on a note row, one on a compact row), Body up to the full note.
- **Browser row** (`NotesBrowserRow`, the Notes window; its words are `NotesBrowserRowParts`): `sm` (12) padding all round (it was `xs` vertical) and `xs` (8) between its lines. Line 1: the marker in a scaled 14 pt column (`relativeTo: .body`), the passage in `.body` semibold `ink.primary` (a conversation's title, one line, then its place and its translation in `.caption2` semibold `ink.secondary`), and at the trailing edge the time (`.dateTime.hour().minute()`, "8:14 AM") under a day heading or the date ("Nov 18, 2026") under a book heading, `.caption` monospaced digits `ink.tertiary`. Under it, at the text's leading edge (not under the marker): the marked words in the page's italic (`quoteFont()`), two lines, on `Color(markup:)` in `ink.primary` when the mark has a colour and on nothing in `ink.secondary` when it has not (a bookmark's label, a colourless anchor, a kept version's words: nothing new is stored, so a kept version has no colour); then up to three lines of the note (`NoteBodyText`, `.callout`, a heading at the row's size), or a conversation's last turn in `.callout` `ink.primary`, two lines; a chapter note has no quote; then the versions mark. VoiceOver reads one element, the kind first and the time last: "Note, John 15:4, 8:14 AM", "Conversation, Mark 4, Outline: Mark 4, 9:03 PM".
- **Group heading** (`NotesGroupHeading`, the Notes window and the phone's Notes tab by date; milestone 11, project 5): the day ("Today", "Yesterday", else the weekday and date, the year added when it is not this year) or the book in `.headline` `ink.primary`, its count trailing in `.callout` `ink.secondary` with monospaced digits, the word following the filter ("2 items", "1 note", "3 highlights", "bookmarks", "conversations", "kept versions"). One VoiceOver element with the header trait ("Today, 2 notes"); never selected and never a keyboard stop. It has no padding of its own: in the Notes window `md` (16) above (`xs` above the first, which with the list's own `xs` makes 16), `xxs` (4) below and `sm` (12) across, so its title aligns with the rows' marker edge.
- **Search rows** (the ⌘K palette; the phone's search keeps the same rules in `PhoneMarkRow`): `xs` (8) vertical and `sm` (12) horizontal padding, no border, no fill but the selection wash.
  - **Group header:** the group's title in `.caption` semibold `ink.secondary`, its count trailing in `.monospacedDigit()`; read as a header, never selected.
  - **Mark row** (a note, highlight, bookmark or conversation): its symbol (`note.text`, `highlighter`, `bookmark.fill` in the accent, `sparkles` in plum), then the reference in `.callout` semibold with the date trailing in `.caption` `ink.tertiary`; beneath, the quoted words in the Quote serif on their highlight color, up to two lines so a late match still shows (primary ink on a fill, secondary without one; a conversation shows none); then the note's first line (a conversation's title) in `.callout` `ink.secondary`. The matched words are bold and underlined in the quote and in the first line alike (`SearchRanker.bolded`; Matt, 2026-11-17: bold alone did not stand out in the results): in the page's serif the face's semibold (`PageFace.settingMatches`; semibold italic in a quote, except Source Serif 4, which has no semibold italic bundled, so a quote's matches there are italic and underlined only; see the Verse row), in SF Pro the system's bold, and a single underline in the text's own ink. No color is added, so the quote keeps its fill and its ink contrast, and on a highlight's fill the underline is the ink's, not the fill's. The underline is an attribute of the text, not a trait, so VoiceOver reads the line as before. VoiceOver reads the kind first ("Note, John 15:4").
  - **Verse row:** `text.quote`, the reference in `.callout`, and when the reader has a colored mark on that verse a 10 pt dot in its color with a 0.5 pt `ink.rule` keyline (VoiceOver hears "Marked"); beneath, the verse in the page's serif (`.callout`, `ink.primary`, two lines) with the matched words semibold and underlined, as in the mark row. The words are the search's own: a bare word matches words that start with it, a quoted phrase its exact words, and an excluded word is never marked. The semibold in each face: Literata SemiBold (SemiBold Italic in a quote; both bundled for this alone, while the page's own semibold titles and headings stay on Medium); Source Serif 4 Semibold (no semibold italic is bundled, so in a quote the matched words are italic and underlined only); Iowan Old Style and Charter their Bold and Bold Italic; New York the system serif's bold.
  - **Other rows** (Go to, a book, a translation): the symbol, the title in `.callout` and a `.caption` `ink.secondary` subtitle.
  - **Recent row** (the ⌘K palette and the phone's search, with nothing typed): the symbol of what it opens (`book` for a chapter read, `text.quote` for a passage looked up, the mark's own symbol for a note, highlight, bookmark or conversation, `magnifyingglass` for a search), the title in `.callout` (the place, the reference or the words searched) and beneath it a `.caption` `ink.secondary` line saying what it was and when ("Read yesterday · ESV", "Looked up Tuesday · KJV", "Note", "Search · 12 verses"). The group's header says "Recent" and carries no count.
  - **Suggestion row** ("Did you mean “abide”?", `magnifyingglass`, its verse count beneath): shown when Scripture found nothing, with no header of its own (the phone's too): it is the only row when nothing else matched, and the last group when a passage, book, translation, command, note or conversation did, so a command typed by name stays first and Return runs it. Return or a click runs that search.
  - **Command row** (⌘K, the Mac only): the symbol, the command's title in `.callout` (the menus' own names; a page style or appearance is one row each, "Page Style: Lectern"), a trailing `checkmark` in `ink.secondary` when it is the one in force, then its shortcut as keycaps on `paper.panel`, read from the shortcuts catalogue (so the two cannot disagree). A command that cannot run now is left out, not dimmed. VoiceOver reads the title, then the shortcut by name, and "Current" for the one in force.
- **Verse-panel rows** (`CompareTranslationsPanel`, `CrossReferencesPanel`; both apps; spec `2026-11-16-milestone-10-on-a-verse-design.md` 4.2 and 5.2): rows in a panel, never cards. Each is a plain view that acts as a button (`versePanelRowAction`: a tap, and on the Mac Return or Space once Tab has reached it), not a `Button`, so that the system focus ring is drawn on the row's rounded shape (radius `md`, 6) and not on a square box; VoiceOver hears a button. A row that cannot open is not one. The row for the translation being read closes the panel and looks nothing up: the reader is already there.
  - **Translation row** (Compare): the translation's short name in the Label style (`.caption` semibold, `ink.secondary`) and, beside it for the one being read, "reading" in `.caption` `ink.secondary`; under them the verse or verses in the page face at `.callout` (roman, `ink.primary`, poetry lines run together as prose, several verses each carrying its number: "12-13 …"). The compared verse is roman and not in the Quote style (the mockup Matt chose from set it so; four whole verses in italic read worse). The row being read sits on `accent.wash` at radius `md`; no other row has a fill. `xs` of padding all round, the rows `xs` apart. A translation that lacks the verse shows one line, "Not in the ERV.", in `.callout` `ink.secondary`, and is not a button. In Psalms the CPDV's row carries one more line in `.caption` `ink.secondary`: "The CPDV numbers the Psalms differently; this may be a neighboring verse." VoiceOver reads the short name (with ", reading" for the one being read), then the verse.
  - **Reference row** (Cross-References): the passage's reference in `.callout` semibold `ink.primary` ("Matthew 5:47-48", the app's own hyphen) over its opening words in the translation being read, in the Quote style (`pageFace.quoteFont()`, `ink.secondary`), one line; a target the translation lacks shows its reference alone. `xs` across and `xxs` down; no fill at rest. VoiceOver reads the reference then the words. Votes are never shown.
  - **Group header** (Cross-References): the group's title ("This verse points to", "Verses that point here") in `.caption` semibold `ink.secondary`, its count trailing in `.monospacedDigit()`, read as a header. Under the short list, "Show all (n)" is a plain `.callout` `accent` button.

- **Versions mark and versions view** (`VersionsMark`, `NoteVersionsView`, `VersionBlock`; spec `2026-11-16-milestone-10-keeping-data-safe-design.md` 4.1 and 4.2; Matt chose the mockup `note-kept-versions.html`): a note that was changed on two devices before they synced shows a **mark** under its text, in the notes column and the Notes window (and on the phone, below). The mark is `square.on.square` and its words ("2 versions") in `.caption` `ink.secondary`, `xs` across and `xxs` down, on `paper.overlay` in a capsule with no stroke: a tonal step, not a chip, not a card, never the accent, never red, never a badge. It is not shown while the note is being edited, and a count of one shows nothing. On the Mac it is a plain button (a scaled 24 pt hit height, help "Show the versions of this note") that opens the system's popover: the versions view, padded `sm`, a scaled 380 pt wide (the content 356 pt, room for a note's three choices in one row at the regular control size) and at most a scaled 420 pt tall, scrolling past that. The view has a title ("Two versions of this note"), a `.callout` `ink.secondary` sentence under it ("It was changed on two devices before they synced. Nothing was lost."), then one **block** for each text: its name and time ("This version · Mac", "Kept version · iPhone"; "Today 8:14 AM", the time in the reader's own region, as every time in the app is), the text itself rendered as a note's is but with no links (a popover over a popover), and under each kept version its choices. **Use This One** (swap: the note takes these words and the other text is kept), **Keep Both in the Note** (the kept text goes under the note's, and the version is removed) and **Discard** (removes the version) are `.bordered` buttons in one row at the regular control size, stacking when they do not fit; Use This One is the system's `.borderedProminent` only while there is one kept version, so nothing is steered when there are several. Neither Discard nor any choice is red and none has a destructive role: **only Discard is undone** (⌘Z, through the window's undo manager, and a shake on the phone), because the other two lose nothing. Blocks are separated by a hairline in `ink.rule`, as the mockup drew, because two texts must not run together. A text whose note is gone is a **text with no note** ("A version with no note", mark "Kept version", help "Show the kept version"): one block with **Restore as a Note** (prominent) and Discard; Restore as a Note puts it back on its passage, and Discard says so in its dialog ("Discard the kept version of John 15:9?"). Once the last version is settled the popover closes by itself. VoiceOver reads the mark as part of its row's label and as an action of the row named "2 versions" (the row is one element); a block is one element, its label the name and time, its value the text, one action for each choice.

### Inputs / Fields

- **Style:** `editorSurface()` (in `ColumnRowParts.swift`) is the one editor surface: `paper.overlay` fill, 1 pt `ink.rule` stroke, radius `md` (6). The note editor, the assistant's composer and a prompt's text in Settings › Prompts all draw it, and each sets its own inner padding (`xs`, 8, in the note editor) around `.body` text. It is the only bordered element inside a row, and the row around it has no border, so it never nests.
- **Focus:** system focus ring. No custom focus color.
- **Markdown toolbar:** five `.plain` symbol buttons (`bold`, `italic`, `textformat.size`, `list.bullet`, `link`) at `.caption`, `ink.secondary`, `xs` apart, above the field.
- **Search** (browser): `.searchable`, system.

### Navigation

- **Front page (the contents page and the pane)** (Library spec 4; `ContentsPage`, `ContentsPageView`, `BookPaneView`, inside `FrontPageView`): one page replaces the old sidebar and shelf. On the left the **contents** on `paper.page`: every book of the translation under its Testament, Old then New, with the Apocrypha (in a translation that has it) as a band of its own below. A Testament's title is in the page face at 1.15 times the reader size with its book count trailing in `.caption` `ink.secondary`, over a 1 pt `ink.rule`; inside it each group (Law, History, Poetry, Major Prophets, Minor Prophets, Gospels & Acts, Paul's Letters, General Letters, Revelation; the Apocrypha is one group) has a **group label** (`GroupLabel`: semibold upper case, `ink.verse`, with the header trait, at 0.58 times the reader size and never under `.caption2`'s size, tracked in proportion; the phone's Library uses the same one at `.caption2` with a scaled 1.4 pt) and then its books, one per row, in the page face at the reader's own page size (`readerFontSize`, 14 to 28, passed in by `MainWindowView`; a change reflows the page at once, with no animation), `ink.primary`. The row's padding, the wash's inset, the dot's gap and the dot scale by the reader size over 18. The page fills as many columns as fit, each as wide as the widest book name of the translation at that size (measured in the page face, once per face, size and set of names, `BookNameMeasure`; the Apocrypha's names count where the translation has it) plus the dot, its gap and the wash's inset on both sides, so no name is ever cut off (ESV and KJV in Literata in a 1,100 pt window with the pane at its narrowest: five at 14, four at 16, three from 18 to 22, two from 24); from four up the two Testaments sit side by side sharing the columns, the Old taking the odd one; with fewer each Testament has the full width, one above the other. A column breaks only before a group, and the breaks are chosen so the tallest column is shortest, then the most even, then the earliest. ## 6. Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** put every color in `Palette.swift` as a `ColorToken` with light and dark values (and Increase Contrast values for ink, rules and accents), reach it through `extension Color` (`.paperPage`, `.inkSecondary`, `.accentWash`) or `.platformColor`, and add the pairing to `PaletteTests`. `MarkupColor` hex pairs remain the storage format for user highlights.
- **Do** take the reader's measure, verse numbers and paragraph spacing from `ReaderTypography.scaled`, so a line holds about 70 characters at 14 and at 24.
- **Do** use the page face only for scripture: chapter text, book title and subtitle, section headings, chapter numerals, quoted phrases. Use SF Pro semantic styles for everything else. Take the face from the reader's page style, never a font name at a call site.
- **Do** show selection as an `accent.wash` fill at radius 6, continuous, with text colors unchanged.
- **Do** separate rows with rhythm (`sm` 12 vertical, `xs` 8 internal) and panes with a `Divider` in `ink.rule`.
- **Do** use `AppSpacing` (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64) and `AppRadius` (4, 6, 10, 12, 14) from `Metrics.swift` by name at every call site; mark text-relative ones `@ScaledMetric`. Highlight fills keep their own 2 pt radius and the focus outline 4 pt (fill plus its 2 pt outset), so the two stay concentric.
- **Do** make every delete undoable through the window's `UndoManager` (`MarkupStore.delete(_:)` returns what `restore(_:)` puts back) rather than asking for confirmation in the reader.
- **Do** keep `ContentUnavailableView` for every empty collection, with one line of instruction.
- **Do** give every symbol button a `.help` and an `.accessibilityLabel`, and a 24 pt minimum hit area.
- **Do** keep the ⌘K panel the only floating surface and the only shadow (the loupe's apart), solid paper with no material and nothing dimmed behind it.
- **Do** reserve plum for the assistant and system red for destruction; verdigris never means either.

### Don't:

- **Don't** use `Color(red:green:blue:)`, `Color(hex:)`, `Color.primary.opacity(...)`, `Color.accentColor.opacity(...)`, `.quaternary` as a fill, `.foregroundStyle(.secondary)`, or system palette colors as tokens. All colors come from `Palette`; a color built from stored hex (a user highlight) is data, not a token.
- **Don't** use `.font(.system(size:))` in chrome. Use semantic text styles. The Dynamic Type Contract names the only exceptions: the reader, the verse numbers and the page style's headings, which derive from the reader size; the Mac front page's book names, Testament titles, chapter numerals and group labels, which derive from it too; and the count inside the chapter-note mark, a `@ScaledMetric(relativeTo: .title)`.
- **Don't** draw the gray-fill-plus-hairline card (`opacity(0.04)` fill with an `opacity(0.08)` stroke at `cornerRadius(8)`) on a note, a compact row or anything else. Rows have rhythm, not boxes.
- **Don't** nest a bordered editor inside a bordered container. The editor is the only bordered element in a row.
- **Don't** make the reader feel like a web page in a window: no full-width text, no flat white, no `Text` of a whole chapter in a `ScrollView`; the TextKit column with measure, insets and baseline rhythm is the reader on every platform.
- **Don't** build a SaaS dashboard: no gradient accents (the app icon's field is the one exception), no identical card grids, no stat tiles, no `.ultraThinMaterial` as texture, no hero metric.
- **Don't** add shadows, springs, bounces or staggered reveals. Motion is `.snappy` or `.smooth` on opacity and fill, and a cut under Reduce Motion. The exceptions are the book thumb's loupe, which springs in and out with the drag (section 5, Book thumb), and the chapter switcher, whose page springs to rest at the lift's speed and whose cards fan and flip on springs (section 5, Chapter switcher).
- **Don't** put a saturated color on the page that is not a highlight, a focused-highlight outline or a bookmark glyph. (The ring round a marked chapter wears the highlights' hues in the Library's cells, not on the page: The One Saturated Voice Rule.)
- **Don't** float a card, a callout or a sheet over the map. Nothing floats in this system but the ⌘K panel, and a card over the map would need a surface and a shadow that are not in section 4: a place's page sits under the map, and the phone's list is the system's sheet. A chapter's dots are the map's own content, never views over it. The one thing laid over the map is the chosen place's name (The chosen label, above): a plate just large enough to read on the tiles, pinned to the place's point and moving with it, not a card.
- **Don't** draw a chart that is the only way to its information. The timeline chart is the book's whole span at a glance, hidden from VoiceOver and out of the Tab order, and everything it shows is a row of the rail beside it; the phone has the rail alone.
- **Don't** write a relation the data does not record. The data has no sex and no birth order, so the app never writes "son", "daughter", "brother", "sister", "eldest", "youngest" or "grandfather" (it says "partner", "sibling", "child", "grandparent"); "father" and "mother" are the data's own and may be said. A label the data stores ("son of Boaz") is shown as stored.
- **Don't** show a year without its line. A date is always followed by the notice (the first time) or the footnote ("Dates follow a traditional chronology."), and with Show dates off no year the app writes appears at all.
- **Don't** colour a missed week or badge a count. A week the group has passed with nothing ticked looks like every other week: no red, amber or grey, no "behind", no badge on the Plan tab, no count in the Dock, no streak. The plan is a record of what was read, not a debt, and the group moves on together.
- **Don't** announce a clash. Two texts that were changed before two devices synced are shown by the quiet versions mark and nothing else: no alert, no badge, no count on a tab or in the Dock, never red and never the accent on the mark or on the choices' text. Nothing was lost, so nothing is an emergency; the reader settles it when they next look, or never. Discard is not red and has no destructive role anywhere, because Undo brings it back.
- **Don't** draw the chapter ring as a circle: it cannot clear a three-figure number. It is the cell's rounded rectangle, inset.
- **Don't** use `.hierarchical` or `.multicolor` symbols, or mix symbol weights on one surface.
- **Don't** reinvent a system control: `Picker`, `Toggle`, `Stepper`, `Slider`, `.searchable`, `NavigationSplitView`, system toolbar and popovers are the vocabulary.
- **Don't** type a padding, size or radius by feel (`10`, `14`, `5`, `56`). If a value is not on the spacing or radius scale and not a `@ScaledMetric`, it is wrong.
