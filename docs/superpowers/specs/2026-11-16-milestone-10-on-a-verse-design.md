# Milestone 10, project 3: on a verse

Status: approved by Matt, 2026-11-16. Third of milestone 10's six projects. Branch `milestone-10-study-tools`. Builds after project 1 (a selected verse, the C and R keys) and project 2 (the Bible database's version 3).

## 1. Intent

Two things a reader does at the desk with a verse in front of them, each one action from the selection:

- **Compare translations.** Matt: "I definitely want this. I'd like it to be an option when selecting a verse. Maybe the user selects which translations to compare against or they have a default for comparisons so we are not always comparing 10+ translations... and then on top of that they can also ask AI to dig into it some more."
- **Cross-references.** Matt: "If someone selects the verse I'd like to show them what references it or what it is referencing."

They are two separate panels. Matt turned down one panel with two tabs: "I don't see why the comparison panel would be grouped with the references."

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**

- A verse beside itself in the reader's chosen translations, in one action.
- The verses a verse points to, and the verses that point to it, strongest first, each one click from being read.
- The assistant can be asked about either, with exactly what is on screen.

**Non-goals**

- Reading side by side in columns. This is the single-verse view only.
- Marking the words that differ between translations.
- Marks in the text showing which verses have cross-references (nearly all do).
- Cross-references for the Apocrypha; the data covers the 66 books.
- Editing, adding or voting on cross-references.

**Anti-goals**

- A wall of translations by default.
- Weak links crowding out strong ones.
- A panel that takes the reader's place: opening a row is a lookup, and Back returns.

## 3. Approaches

**Together or apart.** One panel with Translations and References tabs (mockup `verse-compare-references.html`), or two panels with a menu item each. Matt chose two.

**Where the cross-references come from.** OpenBible.info's cross-reference file (about 345,000 links with votes, CC BY 4.0, drawn chiefly from the public-domain Treasury of Scripture Knowledge). The three repositories Matt named all lead to it. scrollmapper's copies are older, doubled, and lose a target's range; trekhleb's repository pins the unmodified September 2026 file. Chosen: OpenBible's own file, pinned by digest and fetched at build time as the eBible files are, and written into the Bible database by the importer.

**Where the panels show.** On the Mac, a popover on the verse or the inspector (where notes and the assistant are). Chosen: a popover, so the notes column stays as it is. Every popover in the app today hangs from a SwiftUI view (the notes column shows a passage that way); one anchored to a range of the page's text is new work. On the iPhone, a sheet.

## 4. Compare Translations

### 4.1 Opening it

- **Compare Translations** in the selection menu on both apps, and C on the Mac with a selection in the reader.
- It compares the selection's verses: one verse, or a run of verses in one chapter. Part of a verse counts as the whole verse. More than five verses shows the first five and says so in one line. **(call)**

### 4.2 The panel

- **Header:** the reference ("John 15:5") and, trailing, how many translations are shown.
- **Rows:** the translation being read first, on `accent.wash`, marked "reading"; then the reader's compare set in the translation list's order. A row is the translation's short name in the Label style (`.caption` semibold, `ink.secondary`) over the verse in the Quote serif at `.callout`, `ink.primary`, set as prose (poetry lines run together). Several verses carry their numbers.
- A translation that lacks the verse shows one line, "Not in the ERV." in `ink.secondary`, and is not clickable.
- The CPDV numbers the Psalms differently from the others, and the app has no table of which verses differ. In Psalms its row carries one line under the verse, "The CPDV numbers the Psalms differently; this may be a neighbouring verse." in `.caption` `ink.secondary`. Elsewhere it is shown like any other. **(call; a known limit, not a fix.)**
- **A click on a row** shows the verse in that translation as a lookup and closes the panel.
- **Foot:** Ask About the Differences (`sparkles`, in the assistant's plum), Copy, and Translations… at the trailing end.

### 4.3 The compare set

- **Translations…** is a menu of every translation with a checkmark on each one in the set. The same checklist is in Settings › General under "Compare with".
- It starts as ESV, NASB, KJV and WEB. The translation being read is always shown, whether or not it is in the set.
- It is one preference, `compareTranslationIDs`, and travels through the iCloud key-value store with the other taste settings.

### 4.4 Ask and Copy

- **Ask About the Differences** opens the assistant on the verses shown (five at most) and sends the existing Compare translations prompt as the reader has it, edited or hidden or not. The other translations it sends are the rows shown, in their order, not every translation; the on-device model still receives at most three, the first three shown. The panel closes.
- The same prompt run from the assistant's own list now sends the compare set too, in place of every translation (on-device, its first three; today that is the first three by name: AMP, ASV, CPDV). **(call)**
- **Copy** puts every row on the pasteboard, each as Copy with Reference writes it (`“text” — John 15:5 (KJV)`), one to a line.

## 5. Cross-References

### 5.1 Opening it

- **Cross-References** in the selection menu on both apps, and R on the Mac.
- The panel is about one verse: the selection's first. Chevrons beside the reference step to the previous and the next verse of the chapter, so a passage can be walked verse by verse. **(call)**

### 5.2 The panel

- **Header:** the reference with the two chevrons.
- **This verse points to:** the passages this verse's links name. **Verses that point here:** the verses whose links name this verse, or a range that contains it. Each group has its count, strongest first by the data's votes.
- Each group shows up to six rows with at least four votes (OpenBible's own default cut-off), then **Show all** with the count, which lists every link kept. **(call)**
- **A row** is the passage's reference in `.callout` semibold ("Galatians 5:22–23") over its opening words in the translation being read, one line, in the Quote serif, `ink.secondary`. Votes are not shown.
- **A click on a row** shows the passage inside the panel, as a reference in a note does today: the verses, a way back to the list, and Open (Open in Read on the iPhone), which is a lookup.
- A link whose target runs into another chapter (655 of them) is shown and opened as the part in its first chapter, since a reference in this app names one chapter. **(call)**
- Read in the CPDV, a Psalm's opening words may be a neighbouring verse's, for the reason in 4.2; they are shown anyway. **(call; known limit.)**
- A verse with no links shows one line: "No cross-references for this verse."
- **Foot:** Ask About These (plum), and the credit "Cross-references: OpenBible.info" in `.caption` `ink.secondary`, which opens their page.

### 5.3 Ask About These

Opens the assistant on the verse and sends one question the app composes: the verse, then the passages in the two short lists with their text, and the request to explain how each relates to the verse. In the conversation the reader's turn reads "Explain these cross-references", as a prompt's title does. It is not a prompt in Settings › Prompts: a stored prompt would also be listed under Ask About This Passage, where no cross-references have been gathered, and its natural key is already the chapter prompt "Find cross-references". **(call: Matt asked for Ask on the comparison; this gives References the same, and nothing new is stored.)** The usual routing applies: too long for the on-device model, it goes to a cloud provider or says so.

### 5.4 The data

- **Source:** OpenBible.info's `cross_references.txt`, the snapshot dated 2026-09-21, fetched by a new `Scripts/fetch-crossrefs.sh` that checks a pinned SHA-256 digest and stops if the file changed, as `fetch-ebible.sh` does. It is cached outside the repository and never committed.
- **In the Bible database** (schema version 4): one table of links, each a source verse, a target start and end (a target may be a range, and 655 run into another chapter), and its votes, indexed both ways so "points here" is as quick as "points to".
- **Kept:** links with at least one vote. Zero and negative votes (about 3,500) are dropped. **(call)**
- **Numbering:** the data follows the KJV's verse numbers. `3John.1.15` is read as 1:14. A target the reading translation lacks still shows its reference, with no opening words.
- **Size:** about 6 to 14 MB added to the 66 MB database, depending on the indexes; the builder records the measured figure.
- **Credit:** in the panel's foot and in About: "Cross-references from OpenBible.info, CC BY 4.0, drawn chiefly from the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge. Changes: re-keyed to this app's verse table; links with no votes left out." with links to the source and the licence.

**Task 4: the fetch script, the importer and schema 4** (5.4). Measured on OpenBible's `cross_references.txt` (header 2026-09-21; text digest `d18f0cdee1fd9a0bb289e0c30154485857b396124f68fe0dcc37aaa5982644f8`, 8,302,424 bytes): 344,799 links; 3,517 with a vote of zero or less dropped (the spec's "about 3,500"), **341,282 kept**. Of the kept targets, 253,842 are single verses, 86,791 are ranges inside a chapter, 631 run into a later chapter of the book (one as far as six on) and **18 into the next book: 649 leave their chapter** (the spec's 655 before the drop). No source is a range and no pair repeats. Five lines name `3John.1.15`; read as 1:14 they collide with nothing. 66 links' targets hold their own source. John 3:16 points to 23 passages (all at four votes or more) and 104 verses point to it (73 at four or more). **The layout was measured, then chosen:** a verse is one integer (`order × 1,000,000 + chapter × 1,000 + verse`; John 3:16 is 43003016); `cross_reference(source, target_start, target_end, votes)` is `WITHOUT ROWID` with the source leading the key, one index on the target and a partial index on the 649 that cross a chapter.

| Layout | Added to the 69,177,344-byte database |
|---|---|
| A row-id table and two indexes | 18.5 MB |
| The table with the votes in the target's index | 14.1 MB |
| **`WITHOUT ROWID`, source-led key, a target index, a partial index on the 649 (built)** | **13,430,784 bytes (13.4 MB)** |

**As built, the database went from 70,045,696 to 83,476,480 bytes** at schema version 4 (69,177,344 is the size before project 2's word tables, which the table above was measured on; the same 13,430,784 bytes landed on the 70,045,696 that was on disk). The spec said "6 to 14 MB"; the build is at the top. **Which address gave the file:** OpenBible's own address (`https://a.openbible.info/data/cross-references.zip`) now serves a newer file (header 2026-11-16, 8,303,425 bytes, digest `6d5dd56147d3a145663074af69560633087c68f8a291a9c675934fe2b4c6201b`), which the digest rejects. The pinned 2026-09-21 text came from the mirror the plan names (trekhleb's repository on GitHub, its `master`), which is the script's second address (named by its `master` branch when built; since the wave, by the commit `23bf0908…`).
