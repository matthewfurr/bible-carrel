# Milestone 10, project 2: finding

Status: approved by Matt, 2026-11-16. Second of milestone 10's six projects. Branch `milestone-10-study-tools`. Builds after project 1 (verse basics), whose shortcuts catalogue it reads.

## 1. Intent

Two things Matt asked for, which meet in one place:

- **Better search.** Today the whole query is one exact phrase, so "remain fruit" finds nothing. It should match every word in any order, match the start of words, and understand "exact phrases", -excluded words, a scope (Old or New Testament, or one book) and a misspelling ("did you mean").
- **A better ⌘K.** Today the whole window dims, the panel with it, and with nothing typed the panel is an empty field. Matt: "I would like some recents or default options to show up in the dropdown. Think of how Raycast or even Claude Code show their launcher." He approved the whole proposal on 2026-11-16 (mockup `mac-palette-proposal.html`): "Add it all."

The iPhone's search screen takes the same search, since both read one model.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**

- A reader who half-remembers a verse finds it from two or three of its words.
- ⌘K is useful before a key is pressed, and everything the menus do can be run from it.
- The palette teaches the shortcuts: each command shows its key.
- Search never blocks typing.

**Non-goals**

- Searching text that is not on the device. Online translations are out of this milestone; the search takes its translation as a parameter so a fetched text kept on the device could be indexed later.
- Searching several translations at once.
- Typed scope words (`in:romans`). Scope is a control.
- Ranking by how often or how recently a row was chosen.
- Synced recents. They stay on each device and are not in the iCloud store.
- Commands on the iPhone's search screen; the phone has no command list today and gains none.

**Anti-goals**

- A search that guesses so loosely the verse wanted is buried: every word must be present.
- A palette that looks like a dashboard: no tiles, no cards, no second shadow.

## 3. Approaches

**Search.** (1) Keep today's stemmed full-text index and add `*` to each word. (2) Rebuild the index without stemming, so it holds the words as written, and build a real query from the typed words. (3) Keep both indexes. (4) Scan the text directly, as michaelshimeles/bible does. Chosen: (2). Tested against today's index, (1) fails: the index stems what is typed before it looks, so `abidi` finds nothing between `abid` and `abiding`, `his` also finds "him" and "high", and `-fear` also drops "feared" and "fearful". With the words as written, a word start, an exact phrase and an excluded word are each exactly what was typed, the bolded matches agree with the results, and the index's own word list serves "did you mean". The cost is that stemming goes: `love` finds love, loved, loves and loveth but not "loving" (`lov` does). (3) would add tens of megabytes to keep that; (4) throws away what is built.

**"Did you mean".** (1) A word list per translation written into the Bible database at build time, compared by edit distance when a search finds nothing. (2) SQLite's spellfix extension. Chosen: (1); spellfix is not in the system's SQLite.

**The panel.** Matt chose A of the mockup: nothing dims; the panel is solid and bright. (B kept the dimming behind a bright panel.)

## 4. The search

### 4.1 What can be typed

| Typed | Finds |
|---|---|
| `remain fruit` | Verses with both words, in any order, anywhere in the verse. |
| `abid` | Words that start with it, letter for letter (abide, abides, abideth, abiding). Every bare word works this way, so results narrow steadily as a word is typed. Case and accents are ignored. |
| `"living water"` | Those words, next to each other, in that order, exactly as typed (case and accents aside). Curly quotes work. A quote left open runs to the end. |
| `love -fear` | Verses with love and without the word fear. `-"phrase"` excludes a phrase. An excluded word is a whole word, not a word start. |
| `beer-sheba` | A hyphen inside a word joins a phrase; it is not an exclusion. |

Operators the index understands (`OR`, `NOT`, `NEAR`, `*`, `:`) typed by hand are plain words, never commands. A query of excluded words alone finds nothing.

The reader's own marks in ⌘K and the phone's search (`SearchRanker`) already match every word and word starts; they gain phrases and exclusions, over exactly the text they search today: a note's body and quoted words, a highlight's quoted words, a conversation's title and last line. Bookmarks still match by passage alone. The Notes window's own search field is a different search (it also matches inside words and by reference) and is not changed. **(call)**

### 4.2 Scope

A menu beside the results: **Whole Bible** (the default), **Old Testament**, **New Testament**, **Apocrypha** (only in a translation that has it), **This Book** (the book being read, by name) and **Book**, a submenu of every book in canon sections. It limits the Scripture group alone. It returns to Whole Bible each time the palette opens (as built, also when the field is emptied, on both apps: see section 11); on the phone, whose search keeps its query and results while the reader visits one, it returns to Whole Bible when the field is cleared. **(call)**

### 4.3 Order and counts

Scripture results come best match first, as today. The group's count is the number of matching verses in the scope. On the iPhone, the full list behind Scripture's chip gains an order menu, **Best match** and **Bible order**, as the Notes tab has. **(call)**

### 4.4 Did you mean

When Scripture finds nothing, and the query has no quotes and no exclusions, the app looks for a near spelling of each word that is not in the translation's word list, tries the search again with the best candidates, and offers the first that finds something as a row at the top: **Did you mean "abide"?** with its verse count. Return or a click runs it. Nothing is offered when no candidate finds a verse.

Nearness: one changed letter for a word of up to three letters, two for four to six, three for seven or more; closer first, then the commoner word.

### 4.5 Speed

- The search runs off the main thread; a newer keystroke abandons an older search's result.
- It reads only the chosen translation and scope, not all eight translations filtered afterwards as today.
- Budget on Matt's Mac, in a debug build: a common word ("lord") in the whole Bible returns its first rows and its count within 50 ms; measured and recorded in the plan.

### 4.6 The Bible database

Schema version 3. `Scripts/ensure-bible.sh` already rebuilds `Build/bible.sqlite` when the version changes, so a build after pulling picks it up. The full-text table is rebuilt without stemming (the words as written, case and accents folded), with whatever prefix indexes and shape the builder needs for 4.5. "Did you mean" needs each translation's words with how many verses have each; whether that is the index's own vocabulary or a small table is the builder's. Nothing in the synced store changes.

### What was built

**Task 1: the query** (4.1). One value, `SearchQuery`, holds what was typed and every rule about it: the words, the phrases, the exclusions, what the index is asked and which words to bold. Text the index would take as a command (`OR`, `NOT`, `NEAR`, `*`, `:`) is only ever a word. **(call)** An excluded word is checked in every field of a note or highlight, while the words found must all sit in one field. If wrong: a note whose body has "love" and whose quoted verse has "fear" is hidden by `love -fear`, which a reader might not expect. **(call)** Bolding covers each matching whole word once, in text order, so "Abiding" is bold whole and each word of a phrase is its own bold range. If wrong: a word start is bold whole, not only the letters typed. **(call)** "Did you mean" is not offered when there are no words, when a quote was typed, or when any word is excluded; a dash that was dropped (`- fear`) is not an exclusion. If wrong: a reader who types a lone dash gets no suggestion. **(call) the apostrophe rule:** `peter's` or `don’t`, straight or curly, is its pieces side by side with the last one whole, so it finds the possessive and not "Peter said" (the index cuts the word in two, so a plain word start would find both). If wrong: a rare word with an apostrophe in the middle must be typed as the Bible spells it. A leading apostrophe (`'tis`) is an ordinary word start. **(call)** Private-use characters count as word characters (the plan said so, and the index does the same); they are always inside quotes, so they are safe, but "only letters and digits reach the index" is not literal. If wrong: nothing a reader can see. **(call)** The word's memberwise initialiser stays internal; tests reach it. If wrong: nothing. Known and not fixed: a `-` straight after a closing quote (`"living water"-fear`) excludes the next word, against rule 3's "at the start or after white space"; no test pins it.

**Task 2: schema 3, the importer and the store's search** (4.2, 4.3, 4.5, 4.6). Each translation has its own full-text table of the words as written, folded for case and accents, with no stemming. Scope is a run of verse numbers: the importer writes verses in translation, then canon, order, so a testament, the Apocrypha or one book is one unbroken run, and `translation_book` records each book's first and last. The final review checked the real database read-only: all 550 book ranges are whole, with no gap, in all eight translations. The index was chosen against the alternatives by measuring in `sqlite3` on Matt's Mac, on the real text (eight translations, 259,393 verses), warm. **The sizes and times in this table were estimates made in `sqlite3` before the build.** "Rows" is the best twenty by rank; "count" is the total.

**Task 3: did you mean** (4.4). If wrong: a rare double read, with the same result. **(call)** A translation with no word list is kept as an empty one. If wrong: a list added later is not seen until the app restarts. **(call, replaced)** Task 3 tried every combination of near spellings and recorded "fine for typed queries". **That call was wrong for a pasted passage:** the final review (C1) found that a query of many unfamiliar words built 3 to the power of that many combinations (twelve misspelt words took 52 seconds and thirteen 150 seconds on that code, and a pasted chapter would have used all the memory).
