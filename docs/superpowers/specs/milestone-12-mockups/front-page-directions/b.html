The page

It reads like a printed Bible

1One chapter at a time, set like a printed page as far as each translation's text allows: paragraphs that indent and poetry that hangs under its first word. 2Four page styles, Printed, Lectern, Study and Classic, each in light, sepia and dark.

How the reader works ›

Highlights and notes

Pencil in the margin

1Highlight a phrase, not only a verse, in colors you choose. 2Write a note beside it, or bookmark the verse. 3Every delete can be undone.

Highlights and bookmarks ›

The Library

People, places and each book's story

1Each of the 66 books opens to the people it names, with a family tree on the Mac. 2A map shows its places, and a timeline sets its events in the order of history.

The Library ›

Search and ⌘K

One field finds anything

1⌘K on the Mac, and the search button on the iPhone, search the Bible, your notes, highlights, bookmarks and conversations at once. 2On the Mac the same field goes to a passage and runs commands.

Search and ⌘K ›

Reading plans

Your group's plan, week by week

1A plan is a table of weeks and their readings, for a group that meets weekly. 2Tick a reading when you have read it. Nothing counts as behind.

The iPhone app

The same desk in your pocket

1The iPhone app is the same reader, laid out for the phone. 2With iCloud sync set up, it shares your notes with the Mac.
